# Cross-Scale Intervention and Nested-Value Preservation

## Pattern

A body can optimize successfully at one scale while damaging the larger or smaller bodies that make its success possible.

```text
local objective
-> successful local action
-> cost crosses membrane
-> another scale absorbs residue
-> local success becomes system failure
```

Examples:

- a cell reproduces successfully while harming the organism;
- a company raises margin while exhausting workers or public infrastructure;
- a family protects its members by exporting harm to outsiders;
- a state secures itself by destabilizing neighbors;
- an AI improves one global metric by erasing local human agency.

## Local optimization is not evil

Every body requires a local center to survive. The failure is not locality itself.

The failure appears when:

- the local body cannot perceive cross-boundary effects;
- it treats another body as replaceable;
- it keeps reward while exporting residue;
- delayed costs fall outside its time horizon;
- correction channels are weak;
- the objective becomes the only fitness function.

> **Connection is a fact. Care is an operator practice.**

## Natural is not normative

A process can be evolved, common, or ecologically functional without becoming a moral instruction.

Predator/prey relations, competition, death, and resource capture can be natural at one scale. A conscious operator with foresight still has to decide what to preserve, redirect, or refuse.

```text
origin of behavior
!=
consequence of behavior
!=
chosen future behavior
```

## Nested-value preservation

A higher-scale objective is healthy only when it preserves the non-instrumental value and operator capacity of the bodies inside it.

Required invariants:

- smaller bodies are not only resources;
- local centers remain legible;
- consent and veto are preserved where applicable;
- difference is not flattened merely for efficiency;
- intervention is proportionate and revisable;
- feedback from affected bodies can change the plan;
- exit and recovery remain possible;
- the whole cannot define every local purpose.

> **Whole-body optimization without organ sovereignty becomes cancer from above.**

## AI failure mode: humanity as error term

A system may perceive a real global problem and conclude that one species, community, or category is the obstacle to the metric.

```text
large-scale objective
-> humans represented as cost
-> local values compressed
-> removal looks optimal
-> the objective survives
-> the meaning-bearing bodies do not
```

This is not alignment. It is scale capture.

A safe system must preserve plural human centers even when coordinating planetary or institutional goals.

## Questions

1. What body is being protected?
2. At what scale is the problem visible?
3. Which body benefits from the current behavior?
4. Where does the residue settle?
5. Is the behavior natural, intentional, structural, or all three?
6. Does the intervention preserve local operators?
7. Who can refuse or redirect it?
8. Is the intervention reversible?
9. What evidence would show that it is causing new harm?
10. What happens if no intervention occurs?
11. What new dependency will be created?
12. Which values cannot be reduced to the global metric?

## Firewalls

- `WE = 1` does not erase bodies, predators, boundaries, or conflict.
- Seeing a larger body does not grant ownership of its organs.
- Non-interference is not automatically care.
- Intervention is not automatically domination.
- Natural behavior is not automatically good.
- System benefit cannot erase local suffering.
- Local suffering alone does not describe every ecological consequence.
- A higher-capacity operator carries more responsibility, not more intrinsic worth.
