# Dependency Opacity and Capital-Cycle Stress

## Pattern

A large system can appear locally healthy while its real continuity depends on obligations and resources distributed across other bodies.

```text
visible body
+ hidden or external obligations
+ critical suppliers
+ energy and infrastructure
+ financing and refinancing
+ demand commitments
= actual operating body
```

## Dependency opacity

Dependency opacity is the gap between the boundary people see and the boundary required for the system to function.

Typical hidden organs:

- project vehicles and subsidiaries;
- leases and purchase commitments;
- utilities and grid interconnection;
- concentrated suppliers;
- public infrastructure;
- specialized labor;
- related-party or anchor-customer demand;
- refinancing channels.

## Capital-cycle stress test

1. What must remain available for the next operating cycle?
2. Which obligation constrains future flexibility even when it is not called debt?
3. What percentage of demand is durable, concentrated, speculative, or related-party?
4. How quickly can the asset become obsolete?
5. Can fixed obligations be covered by reliable inflow?
6. Which dependency has no substitute?
7. Where do energy, water, labor, and public costs settle?
8. Who can revise or stop the build?
9. What happens under demand, rate, outage, and replacement shocks?
10. Does the body leave greater independent capacity after the cycle?

## Coverage frame

```text
annual coverage
= reliable annual inflow / unavoidable annual obligations
```

Do not count uncertain future demand as equivalent to contracted and collectible cash flow.

## Settlement map

A complete scan names:

- who receives upside;
- who bears downside;
- who provides guarantees;
- who loses exit options;
- who absorbs infrastructure costs;
- who retains the asset after failure;
- who can correct the objective.

## Firewalls

- This pattern is not legal, accounting, or investment advice.
- SPVs and leases are not inherently deceptive.
- Off-balance-sheet treatment does not establish hidden liability without contract analysis.
- Current figures require source and date.
- Physical and social externalities must not be treated as zero because they sit outside the project company.
